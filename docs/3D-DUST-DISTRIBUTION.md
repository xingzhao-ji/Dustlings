# Irregular powder distribution experiment

Status: one isolated candidate, awaiting independent visual review and human validation. **Canonical dust code is unchanged.** The candidate is under [builds/3d/experiments/dust-distribution](../builds/3d/experiments/dust-distribution/); it preserves the root's current helper-work batch and feedback methods.

The player benefit is a clearer impression of removable dust instead of printed decoration on stone. Evidence comes from the independent critic's repeated pattern concern and actual full-progression views at approximately 25% and 75% clean. The broad pale marks repeat within individual pavers. Source inspection shows uniformly sampled dust cells, wide drift footprints and placement clamping to flat paver faces. That can bias placement toward tile interiors. It is a mechanism hypothesis, not proof that every mark is centered: the baseline has only 24/219 drift centers within 0.10 world units of a tile center.

The smallest candidate changes only static distribution: broad deterministic world-space clustering, asymmetric position jitter, and slightly smaller maximum footprints so shapes have room to sit off-center without crossing grout. The existing four static batches, all meshes/materials, color values, field sampling/fading, reachable surfaces, brushing, helper-work particles and caps remain. No palette, ground shader, creature or progression edit is included. The [exact patch](../builds/3d/experiments/dust-distribution/candidate.diff) is available for root review; merge only its distribution changes if supported.

## Actual checks and comparison

Headless CPU-submitted geometry checks pass for both sources. Baseline has 997 static instances and 219 drifts; candidate has 623 instances and 153 drifts. Candidate has 5/153 drift centers within 0.10 of a tile center. Both use four static batches, keep every generated vertex inside a flat paver face and preserve the existing feedback API/caps. Candidate height is 0.001–0.023533 world units. These measurements explain bounds and distribution; they are not aesthetic scores or GPU readback.

The native comparison used Godot 4.7.2, Metal Forward+, Apple M4 Pro, 1280 × 800 at 30 MovieMaker FPS, frozen main/simulation/baseline/candidate sources and an isolated QA profile. The [6.8-second silent clip](../builds/3d/experiments/dust-distribution/comparison.mp4) shows matched untouched views, approximately four seconds of unbroken generated brushing at normal engine time, then explicit 50-helper and fully-cleared fixtures. It is not human input, a natural full playthrough, an audio audition or wall-clock performance measurement. Brushing removed 3.428333 dust units. No ordinary progress was accessed.

## Observation and remaining decision

At normal size, this developer observes fewer repeated large stamps and more unevenly placed powder patches. The cleared region still removes marks and reveals the same paving; the fully-cleared candidate leaves the underlying geometry intact. This is a developer visual hypothesis, not the independent critic's judgment or human approval.

The tradeoff remains material: the sparser areas may look like ordinary clean brown paving, and smaller pale marks can still read as decorative stone. This candidate does not establish that players identify dust before instruction. Review the exact matched images/clip before any merge; do not add another palette variant to evade that question.

Acceptance remains: an independent normal-size comparison should find reduced printed repetition without losing the identity of removable mess; human players should identify dust and its removal without coaching. After an accepted merge, check sustained 50-helper frame times and the actual current brushing/crew scene. Exact next action: root and the separate critic compare this single candidate, preserve disagreements, and either merge the focused distribution patch or retain the canonical baseline with a specific unresolved gap. Human sessions and owner approval remain zero.
