# Dustlings — a place to return to

Owner direction on 2026-09-10 supersedes the cleanup-only scope. Current milestone: one integrated third-person RPG neighborhood. It is not complete or human accepted. Root is lead developer; story and world specialists own separate modules; the independent critic owns its verdicts.

## Original direction

A pocket village called Bellwether has stopped meeting across its brook. A traveler moves into the small keeper’s house. Dustlings remember how places were cared for. Brushing is a close, embodied interaction; companions turn care into access and discovery. No combat, daily penalties, relationship decay or compulsory grind.

Four residents: Mara repairs things too readily and asks for help too rarely; Ivo maps shortcuts but has an undelivered parcel; Nell tends what remains and remembers a family melody; Tavi wants a table large enough for neighbors again. The bridge changes daily routes. The garden creates a gathering place. Trust and observation reveal a greenhouse, a remembered promise and a useful light ability. Details and writing are hypotheses pending play.

## Reference decisions

[Stardew Valley’s official description](https://www.stardewvalley.net/about/) connects inhabiting a home, local relationships and restoration. Apply this through several self-chosen small projects, named residents and visible social consequences, not replicated farming or its characters. [Sky’s official presentation](https://www.thatskygame.com/) informs quiet spatial discovery and connection. Dustlings keeps its original paper, sage, clay, warm stone and tiny seedling companions rather than copying Sky’s costumes or realms.

Camera: stable follow at walking height with an elevated view, direct camera-relative movement, deliberate manual orbit, no head bob or forced scenic sweeps. Immediate stop, forgiving collision, optional gentle animation. World: soft real geometry, open readable lanes, enterable cutaway rooms, recognizable silhouettes, source-bound actual gameplay review. Interface: contextual E prompt, concise conversation choices, optional journal, accessible pause/settings. Sound: preserve original motif/spatial cue system; extend arrangements with actual world progress. No listening approval exists.

## First bounded integration cycle

Benefit: the player can occupy the world and choose whom or what to approach. Evidence: the owner’s new requirements; old point-and-brush scene cannot demonstrate an RPG. Smallest useful implementation: one new neighborhood scene, avatar/third-person controller, four spatial residents, state-driven dialogue/projects, home, bridge, garden, greenhouse and companion interaction. Effort: initial modules approximately 15–20 minutes each, followed by integration/correction cycles.

Acceptance: native scene loads; player walks around solid props, enters home, talks without moving underneath dialogue, brushes nearby real surfaces, gains companion, completes projects in varied order, opens bridge and hidden area through earned flags, saves and resumes safely. Film actual inputs and inspect at normal size. Automated routes are not unfamiliar-player sessions. Retain defects rather than claiming a prototype polished.

## Validation still required

Representative cozy RPG players (separate from incremental/cleanup cohorts and developer friends) must assess control comfort, resident appeal, attachment, agency, fair clues, meaningful consequences, surprise and desire to return. Original numerical rules were for the courtyard: retain them as historical, do not pretend they validate the changed RPG. Pre-register opening observation tasks before recruiting 12–20 unfamiliar target players. Owner review is separate. Figma remains unavailable; native UI is the inspectable fallback. Windows hardware, physical controls, audio audition, Steam release and signing gates remain separate.
