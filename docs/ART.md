# Courtyard presentation checkpoint

The visual direction is a hypothesis for human review: a treasured miniature garden built from warm plaster, terracotta, carved limestone, sage leaves, and pale turquoise water. The renderer uses original Canvas artwork and no borrowed game assets.

## Concrete decisions

- Restrained cream, sage, terracotta and turquoise palette. The only saturated area is the restored fountain water, making the landmark a stable visual destination.
- A shallow oblique ground projection, rounded rectangular plinth, large pavers, paired cottages and entrance steps communicate a small model village. Surface detail stays subordinate to characters and destinations.
- One creature design: scalloped cream-gray fluff, dark separated eyes, mint sprout, ochre feet. A contact shadow remains on the ground while the body makes a small walking hop. Working adds a tiny brush; the fountain ritual replaces brushing with singing expressions and sparse musical notes.
- A visible assignment flag persists at the player's chosen destination. The pointer follows the ground perspective. Touch brushing adds a marker above the contact area.
- Restoration removes moss, lightens carved stone, fills the basin, starts streams, and opens small flowers around the rim. Dormant, smaller gray-green planting grows fuller and flowers as cleanup and fountain restoration progress. Ripples and notes respect reduced motion.
- Sprite-like dust and static scenery are cached. The fountain and characters share ground-depth sorting; the static houses and planting remain outside the playable field.

These choices translate the user-provided benchmark qualities into inspectable composition, scale, transformations, silhouettes, friendly objects, and restrained feedback. No claim of matching benchmark quality is made.

## Coordinate and scale contract

Ground projection is `screenX = offsetX + worldX × scale` and `screenY = offsetY + worldY × .72 × scale`. The inverse uses the same scale and offsets. The default view reserves 80 CSS pixels above and 110 below for the interface, adjustable with `setViewportInsets(top,bottom)`. For a CSS-pixel canvas of width W and available height A, `fitScale = min((W−24)/1020, (A−24)/638)`, clamped to at least .1. Initial offsets center the full scene in this available space. `setCamera(zoom,centerX=500,centerY=390)` sets a 1–3× zoom and world-space camera center; it also accepts an object with the same property names. The initial world point (500,390) is the zoom anchor. Camera values persist on resize, and the input transform remains the exact inverse. The scene is clipped to the available rectangle so zoomed artwork does not obscure the reserved interface.

Scene artwork spans approximately x=37…964, projected y=−52…535. Device pixel ratio is capped at 2. Heights of objects are vertical on screen; only their ground locations are projected. Creature body radius is 9.7–11.5 world units, with up to 1.35× optical enlargement on small layouts. Buildings are about 145 units wide; brush radius is supplied by simulation. The fountain basin is 83 units wide from center to edge, matching the current simulation obstacle.

## Actual review and unresolved defects

The renderer passed a JavaScript syntax check and was rendered in headless Chromium at 1200×800. Isolated stills were inspected at normal viewing size before integration. The full plinth, plants, roofs, lanterns and steps fit without clipping. Integrated opening and 50-creature fixture stills were then inspected in locally installed Google Chrome at 1440×900 and a simulated 390×844 phone viewport. The responsive check found and corrected an actual defect: setting an inline CSS canvas size had prevented resizing from desktop to phone. CSS now owns the displayed size; the renderer only updates backing pixels. Desktop and 1.7× phone zoom fixtures retain separated silhouettes in two staggered rings. These are technical/art inspection observations by the assistant, not human playtest findings.

The camera's forward/inverse transform was checked at zoom 1, 1.7 and 3 with an offset center and multiple world boundary points; largest round-trip error was 1.14×10⁻¹³ world units. A 180-call synthetic rendering check with 50 creatures, no dust, DPR 1 and a 1000×650 canvas measured median 1.0ms and p95 1.5ms of JavaScript render submission, with a 749.1ms maximum outlier during the tight synchronous loop. This measures neither sustained frame rate nor a physical device experience. Integrated animation-frame performance testing remains authoritative for the prototype.

Final documentation review inspected [dirty gameplay](../evidence/final-dirty.png), [restored gameplay](../evidence/final-restored.png), [phone fit](../evidence/final-iphone.png), and [phone 1.7× zoom](../evidence/final-iphone-zoom.png). The final stills show the revised dust, dormant-to-bloomed planting, and implemented two-ring arrangement. Completion/crowd captures use controlled saved progress and are not evidence of uninterrupted human play or human pacing.

Remaining presentation questions and corrections:

1. **Observed phone-scale limitation:** whole-field framing makes characters small even with optical enlargement. The fit image leaves substantial vertical space around the miniature village. A 1.7× zoom improves face size and crops outer scenery; panning is implemented. Physical iPhone/iPad comfort, finger occlusion, target visibility, and reading remain unverified.
2. **Dust correction implemented:** the jittered node grid is unchanged, but 12 cached variants, rotation/size variation, and connected mats soften the repeated appearance in the final opening. Human review must still judge visual pleasure and local brushing comprehension; the grid issue is no longer an unaddressed three-variant-art task.
3. **Crowd correction implemented:** the simulation uses two staggered rings at radii 112/150, expanding by 9 on completion. The final restored desktop and zoomed-phone captures show both rings. Dense work, gathering motion, and destination readability still require human observation; do not report the old single-ring arrangement as current.
4. **Garden correction implemented:** final dirty planting is small, gray-green, and flowerless; the final restored garden is fuller and flowered. Whether this reads as meaningful care and restoration remains a player question.
5. **Typography gap remains; control sizes corrected; final smoke verified:** the design targets are 16 px body and 14 px secondary text. Implemented main copy is 14 px, upgrade descriptions 12 px desktop/13 px phone, and some secondary/branch text 10 px. The fallback UI has been enlarged, but these typography targets are not met. The previously smaller short-landscape dock and tablet header controls have received 44 px minimum overrides; root's final artifact smoke will verify them. Normal-size human readability and target tests remain pending.
6. **Completion caption corrected; final smoke verified:** the previously observed “A place waiting to bloom” caption now changes to “A little place, alive again” on completion. Root is refreshing the final captures and checking the exact artifact; no human review is claimed.
7. Canvas shading is deliberately graphic and quiet. Player review should judge tactile quality at actual gameplay size; no aesthetic score is inferred from code or screenshots. Integrated recordings/audio evidence are owned by the main checkpoint; still-image review here does not establish normal-speed animation or native audio audition.

## Focused second art cycle

The root requested an eight-minute correction budget after reviewing the integrated opening. Dust now has 12 cached variants rather than three, with deterministic node-specific rotation and size, connected diffuse mats, and a centered visible tuft. It shrinks as its amount decreases. Dust is clipped to the reachable courtyard ground and rendered behind pots/lanterns, so diffuse spill does not paint over decorative objects. The simulation and node locations remain unchanged. Dormant planting is smaller, gray-green and flowerless; clearing the courtyard and restoring the fountain progressively brings back fuller green leaves and flowers. Two garden caches preserve this effect without per-frame plant construction. The touch direct-preview marker also remains visible while `pointer.preview` is true.

Revised normal-size integrated captures were produced at 1440×900: `/tmp/dustlings-art-cycle2-dirty.png` and `/tmp/dustlings-art-cycle2-restored-fixture.png`. The restored image uses an explicitly synthetic 50-creature state. Assistant visual review observes a stronger dirty/clean difference, less regular pile appearance, and a visible garden bloom. Human appeal remains unmeasured. A 120-frame opening check in headless installed Google Chrome, canvas 1020×727 and DPR 1, returned mean frame interval 16.666ms, p95 16.7ms, p99 16.8ms, and zero intervals over 33.4ms. This is a short local opening check with zero creatures; it does not replace sustained 50-creature, physical-device, or mobile profiling.
